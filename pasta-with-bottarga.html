Pasta con la Bottarga

(Pasta with Bottarga)

Bottarga has deep Mediterranean roots; its name derives from Arabic butārikh, meaning salted fish roe. In Sicily, tuna bottarga is especially associated with places such as Favignana, Trapani, San Vito Lo Capo, and Marzamemi, where tuna preservation has long shaped coastal foodways. Its legacy endures.

Preparation

Pasta with Bottarga

Sicilian pasta with freshly grated tuna bottarga, garlic, chile, parsley, and extra-virgin olive oil.
Yield: 4 servings
Ingredients
1 1/8 lb durum-wheat pasta
1/2 cup freshly grated tuna bottarga
1 garlic clove
1/16 tsp dried chile flakes
2 tbsp finely chopped fresh parsley
1/4 cup plus 2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
For cooking the pasta
5 1/4 qt water
2 tbsp fine salt

Preparation

1. Bring the water and salt to a boil.
2. Cook the pasta until al dente and drain it.
3. Divide the parsley into two 1-tablespoon portions and the bottarga into two 1/4-cup portions.
4. Heat the olive oil in a wide skillet over medium-low heat.
5. Add the garlic, 1 tablespoon parsley, and the chile flakes. Cook for 2 minutes without browning the garlic.
6. Transfer the pasta to the skillet and toss it in the seasoned oil for 1 minute.
7. Turn off the heat completely.
8. Add the second tablespoon of parsley and 1/4 cup bottarga. Toss for 30 seconds.
9. Divide the pasta among 4 plates.

Service

Distribute 1 tablespoon of the remaining bottarga over each plate and serve immediately.

Chef’s Note

Add the bottarga only after the heat is turned off. The residual heat of the pasta is enough to release its aroma without muting its clean marine flavor.

Possible Alterations & Benefits

Finish with lemon zest or a small squeeze of lemon — brightens the seafood and cuts through oil, butter, or tomato richness.
