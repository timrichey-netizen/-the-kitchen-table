Pasta alla Norma

Originating in Catania, this Sicilian classic combines pasta, tomato, fried eggplant, basil, and ricotta salata. Tradition links its name to Vincenzo Bellini’s opera Norma, with one popular story crediting Catanese playwright Nino Martoglio for comparing the dish to the celebrated opera.

Preparation

Pasta alla Norma

Classic Catania pasta with tomato sauce, fried eggplant, ricotta salata, and basil.
Yield: 4 servings
Ingredients
4 cups dry penne or macaroni
2 cups plus 1 tbsp plus 2 tsp tomato sauce
2 medium eggplants
3/4 cup finely grated ricotta salata
1 1/2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
1 garlic clove
1 cup loosely packed fresh basil leaves
4 1/4 cups peanut or sunflower oil for frying
1 2/3 tsp fine salt for the eggplant
1/3 tsp fine salt for the sauce
For cooking the pasta
4 1/4 qt water
1 tbsp plus 1 2/3 tsp fine salt

Preparation

1. Cut the eggplants into slices about 3/8 inch thick.
2. Layer the slices in a colander and distribute the 1 2/3 teaspoons salt evenly over them. Drain for 30 minutes.
3. Rinse the eggplant briefly and dry it completely with paper towels.
4. Heat the frying oil to 350°F. Fry the eggplant in batches for 2–3 minutes per side, until golden with a soft center. Drain on paper towels.
5. Heat the 1 1/2 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil in a saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the garlic and cook for 2 minutes without browning it.
6. Add the tomato sauce, three-quarters of the basil, and 1/3 teaspoon salt. Cook over medium-low heat for 15 minutes.
7. Remove the garlic. Cut half of the fried eggplant into strips and stir it into the tomato sauce. Reserve the remaining slices for serving.
8. Bring the water and salt to a boil.
9. Cook the pasta until al dente and drain it.
10. Transfer the pasta to the tomato-and-eggplant sauce and toss over medium heat for 1 minute.
11. Finely grate the ricotta salata.

Service

Divide the pasta among 4 plates. Top with the reserved eggplant slices, ricotta salata, and remaining basil.

Chef’s Note

Dry the eggplant thoroughly before frying. Oil held at 350°F browns the slices quickly and limits excess oil absorption.

Possible Alterations & Benefits

Roast or air-fry the fried component instead of deep-frying — reduces oil absorption while preserving browning and crisp edges.
