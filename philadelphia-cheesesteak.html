American (Philadelphia)

Philadelphia Cheesesteak

Philadelphia’s iconic hot sandwich of thinly sliced ribeye, griddled onions, and melted cheese packed into a long soft-crusty roll.

American (Philadelphia)Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, United States35 min

Ingredients

  • For 4 cheesesteaks:
  • 1 1/2 lb (680 g) boneless ribeye steak, well chilled
  • 1 large yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 tbsp neutral cooking oil, plus more as needed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, divided, or to taste
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 long Philadelphia-style sandwich rolls, split lengthwise without cutting completely through
  • 8 slices white American cheese
  • Optional traditional cheese substitution:
  • 8 slices mild provolone, OR about 1 cup warm Cheez Whiz, in place of the American cheese

Preparation

  1. Chill the ribeye until firm but not frozen, about 30 minutes if necessary. Slice it across the grain as thinly as possible, then cut any very long slices into manageable pieces.
  2. Heat a large griddle or heavy skillet over medium-high heat. Add the oil and sliced onion. Season with a small pinch of the salt and cook, stirring frequently, until softened and browned at the edges, 6–8 minutes. Transfer the onions to one side of the griddle or to a plate.
  3. Increase the heat to high. Spread the sliced ribeye over the hot cooking surface in a thin layer. Let it sear briefly, then use two spatulas to turn, separate, and roughly chop the meat as it cooks. Season with the remaining salt and black pepper. Cook just until browned and no longer pink, about 3–5 minutes; avoid overcooking.
  4. Return the onions to the beef and toss together. Divide the mixture into 4 portions roughly the length of the rolls.
  5. Lay 2 slices of white American cheese over each portion. Let the cheese melt for 30–60 seconds. If needed, add a teaspoon of water beside the meat and briefly cover to create steam. If using provolone, melt it the same way. If using Cheez Whiz, omit sliced cheese and spoon the warmed Whiz over the beef when assembling.
  6. Open each roll and place it cut-side down over a portion of beef for several seconds to warm it and absorb some of the juices. Slide a spatula beneath the meat and cheese, then turn the filled roll upright so the beef, onions, and melted cheese settle into the bread.
  7. Serve immediately while the beef is hot, the cheese is melted, and the roll remains soft inside with a lightly crusty exterior.
