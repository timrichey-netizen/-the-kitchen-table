Cooking Methods, Ingredients & Best Practices

A practical guide to the techniques, ingredients and habits behind confident cooking. Search the library or choose a topic to explore.

COOKING METHOD

Sautéing

Cook small pieces quickly in a preheated pan with a thin layer of fat

In practiceDry ingredients, avoid overcrowding, and adjust heat to prevent scorching

COOKING METHOD

Searing

Build a flavorful browned surface using direct high heat

In practicePat food dry and allow the first side to brown before turning

COOKING METHOD

Roasting

Cook with surrounding dry oven heat for even browning

In practicePreheat the oven, spread ingredients apart, and check doneness independently of time

COOKING METHOD

Braising

Brown ingredients, then cook gently with some liquid and a lid

In practiceUse enough liquid for moist cooking without submerging everything

COOKING METHOD

Stewing

Simmer smaller pieces submerged or mostly submerged in liquid

In practiceKeep a gentle simmer and add delicate vegetables toward the end

COOKING METHOD

Poaching

Cook delicate food in gently heated liquid below a rolling boil

In practiceUse restrained heat and monitor doneness carefully

COOKING METHOD

Steaming

Cook with vapor from boiling water without immersing food

In practiceKeep food above the water and maintain steady steam

COOKING METHOD

Blanching

Briefly boil ingredients, often followed by rapid cooling

In practiceSalt water when appropriate, work in batches, and drain thoroughly

COOKING METHOD

Grilling

Cook using radiant heat from a grill surface or flame

In practiceSeparate hot and moderate zones and manage flare-ups

COOKING METHOD

Shallow frying

Cook food in a modest depth of hot fat

In practiceControl oil temperature and drain finished food on a rack

COOKING METHOD

Deep frying

Submerge food in hot oil to cook and crisp it

In practiceUse suitable equipment, avoid overfilling, and never add water to hot oil

COOKING METHOD

Emulsifying

Suspend droplets of one liquid in another to make a stable sauce

In practiceAdd oil gradually while whisking or blending; temperature matters

COOKING METHOD

Reduction

Concentrate flavors by evaporating liquid

In practiceUse a wide pan and taste before adding additional salt

COOKING METHOD

Deglazing

Loosen browned pan deposits with liquid

In practiceRemove excess fat first and scrape with a suitable utensil

COOKING METHOD

Resting

Allow cooked food to settle before slicing or serving

In practiceUse appropriate holding temperatures; rest time depends on size and type

INGREDIENT

Salt

Seasons food and affects moisture and texture

In practiceSeason in stages, taste when safe, and account for salty ingredients

INGREDIENT

Cooking oils

Transfer heat and influence flavor

In practiceChoose an oil suited to the method and avoid overheating

INGREDIENT

Butter

Adds rich dairy flavor and supports pan sauces

In practiceControl heat carefully; milk solids brown and eventually burn

INGREDIENT

Garlic

Adds savory aroma from sharp to mellow

In practiceMince for stronger distribution; prevent small pieces from burning

INGREDIENT

Onions and shallots

Build sweetness, aroma and the base of many dishes

In practiceSweat gently for sweetness or brown slowly for deeper flavor

INGREDIENT

Tomatoes

Bring acidity, sweetness, moisture and umami

In practiceBalance concentrated tomato flavors with time, seasoning and fat

INGREDIENT

Fresh herbs

Add volatile fresh aroma and bright finishing flavor

In practiceAdd delicate herbs late; hardy herbs tolerate longer cooking

INGREDIENT

Dried herbs and spices

Provide concentrated flavor from preserved aromatics

In practiceBloom many spices briefly in fat without scorching

INGREDIENT

Vinegar and citrus

Introduce acidity and brighten rich dishes

In practiceAdd in small amounts and taste after mixing

INGREDIENT

Flour and starches

Thicken, structure and bind foods

In practiceDisperse starch correctly and allow enough cooking for proper texture

INGREDIENT

Pasta

Wheat-based staple in varied shapes for different sauces

In practiceUse abundant water as suitable, stir early, and finish in sauce when practical

INGREDIENT

Rice and grains

Provide texture and absorb seasoned liquids

In practiceChoose liquid ratios and agitation based on the variety and dish

INGREDIENT

Eggs

Bind, emulsify, thicken, and set when heated

In practiceUse controlled temperatures to avoid unwanted curdling

INGREDIENT

Cheese

Adds salt, fat, protein and texture

In practiceAdd hard grated cheese off strong heat to reduce clumping

INGREDIENT

Stocks and broths

Form flavorful bases for soups, stews and sauces

In practiceTaste before reducing; salt concentration increases as liquid evaporates

BEST PRACTICE

Mise en place

Organize equipment and ingredients before cooking

In practiceRead the full recipe, measure ingredients, and prepare pans in advance

BEST PRACTICE

Knife safety

Cut efficiently with stable food and controlled hand placement

In practiceKeep knives sharp, use a stable board, and secure round ingredients

BEST PRACTICE

Cross-contamination

Keep raw and ready-to-eat foods separate

In practiceUse separate utensils and clean surfaces after handling raw proteins

BEST PRACTICE

Food temperatures

Judge food safety with measured internal temperature where needed

In practiceUse a calibrated thermometer and current official food-safety guidance

BEST PRACTICE

Heat management

Control cooking intensity rather than following time alone

In practiceObserve sound, aroma, browning and moisture, then adjust burner power

BEST PRACTICE

Seasoning balance

Balance salt, acid, fat, sweetness, bitterness and savoriness

In practiceTaste incrementally when safe; change one variable at a time

BEST PRACTICE

Texture control

Manage moisture, surface drying and timing

In practiceDo not overcrowd pans and use racks to preserve crispness

BEST PRACTICE

Ingredient substitutions

Swap based on function, not just taste

In practiceConsider water, fat, acidity, thickening and allergens when replacing items

BEST PRACTICE

Allergen awareness

Identify declared allergens and assess ingredient labels

In practiceA missing allergen listing is not proof of allergen-free preparation

BEST PRACTICE

Food storage

Cool, cover, refrigerate and reheat foods safely

In practiceFollow current USDA/FDA storage rules, including the danger-zone guidance

BEST PRACTICE

Meal timing

Coordinate long-cooking components with fast finishes

In practiceStart slow dishes first, and prepare garnishes just before serving

BEST PRACTICE

Recipe scaling

Adjust batch sizes while accounting for cookware and heat transfer

In practiceScale ingredient amounts systematically but recalculate cooking times
