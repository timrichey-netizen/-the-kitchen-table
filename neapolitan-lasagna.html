Lasagna Napoletana

Neapolitan Lasagna

A rich specialty of Naples, this lasagna is closely tied to Carnival, especially Shrove Tuesday, when extravagant meat-and-cheese dishes traditionally preceded Lent. Unlike the Bolognese version, it is built with ragù, ricotta, mozzarella, tiny meatballs, and often eggs or salami.

An extravagant baked centerpiece layered with slow-cooked ragù, creamy ricotta, melting cheese, tender lasagna, miniature meatballs, and hard-boiled eggs. Every slice is deeply savory, luxurious, and festive—the kind of dish designed for a long family meal.

Preparation

NEAPOLITAN LASAGNA
Rich Neapolitan lasagna with egg pasta sheets, slow-cooked meat sauce, tiny meatballs, ricotta, fiordilatte, hard-boiled eggs, and Parmigiano Reggiano.
Yield: 8 servings
Ingredients
For the pasta sheets
1 2/3 cups type 00 flour
1 1/4 cups fine durum-wheat semolina
4 large eggs
For the veal rolls and meat sauce
1 1/8 lb thin veal cutlets
1/4 cup finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano
1/4 cup finely chopped fresh parsley
6 fresh mint leaves, finely chopped
1 garlic clove, finely minced
2/3 lb fresh pork pancetta, cut into pieces
6 1/3 cups tomato passata
3/4 cup dry white or red wine
1 medium onion, diced
1 medium carrot, diced
1 celery stalk, diced
1/3 cup plus 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp extra-virgin olive oil
1 2/3 cups water
2 tsp fine salt
For the meatballs
1 1/8 lb ground veal or a veal-and-pork mixture
1 large egg
1/3 cup plus 1 tbsp finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano
1/4 cup finely chopped fresh parsley
3/4 tsp fine salt
4 1/4 cups peanut oil for frying
For assembly
4 hard-boiled eggs
1 1/8 lb well-drained fiordilatte or scamorza
2 cups ricotta
1 cup finely grated Parmigiano Reggiano
For cooking the pasta
6 1/3 qt water
2 tbsp plus 1 tsp fine salt
Preparation
  • Make the veal rolls. Divide the 1/4 cup Parmigiano, 1/4 cup parsley, mint, and garlic among the veal cutlets. Roll each cutlet tightly and secure it with kitchen twine.
  • Make the meat sauce. Heat the olive oil in a large heavy pot over medium heat. Add the onion, carrot, and celery and cook for 8 minutes, stirring, until softened without browning.
  • Add the veal rolls and pancetta. Brown for 10 minutes, turning the meat so all sides color evenly.
  • Pour in the 3/4 cup wine and cook for 3 minutes, until the sharp alcohol aroma has largely cooked away.
  • Add the 6 1/3 cups passata, 1 2/3 cups water, and 2 teaspoons salt. Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat. Cook uncovered for 60 minutes over medium-low heat.
  • Reduce the heat to low and continue cooking for another 3 hours, stirring every 20–30 minutes. After about 2 hours of total cooking time, remove the veal rolls and pancetta and reserve them. Continue cooking the sauce until concentrated but still fluid.
  • Make the pasta dough. Combine the type 00 flour and semolina, form a well, and add the 4 eggs. Knead for 10 minutes until smooth and elastic. Cover and rest for 60 minutes.
  • Roll the dough through a pasta machine to setting No. 5, or to about 1/16 inch thick. Cut sheets to fit a roughly 13 x 9-inch baking dish. Lay them on clean towels and dry for 2 hours.
  • Make the meatballs. Combine the ground meat, egg, 1/3 cup plus 1 tablespoon Parmigiano, 1/4 cup parsley, and 3/4 teaspoon salt. Form small meatballs about 3/4 inch in diameter.
  • Heat the peanut oil to 350°F. Fry the meatballs in batches for 2–3 minutes, until lightly golden rather than deeply browned. Drain on paper towels.
  • Bring the 6 1/3 quarts water and 2 tablespoons plus 1 teaspoon salt to a boil. Cook the pasta sheets a few at a time for 1–2 minutes. Drain and lay flat on dry kitchen towels.
  • Slice the 4 hard-boiled eggs. Cut the fiordilatte or scamorza into 3/8-inch cubes. Stir 2/3 cup of the meat sauce into the 2 cups ricotta until creamy.
  • Preheat the oven to 350°F.
  • Spread 1 cup meat sauce over the bottom of the baking dish. Cover with one layer of pasta sheets.
  • Distribute portions of meat sauce, ricotta, fiordilatte, hard-boiled egg, meatballs, and Parmigiano over the pasta. Repeat in the same order until there are 5 pasta layers total, dividing the fillings evenly among the layers.
  • Finish the top layer with meat sauce and Parmigiano.
  • Bake at 350°F for 55–60 minutes, until heated through and lightly browned on top.
  • Rest for 20 minutes before cutting.
    • Service
    Cut into 8 portions and serve hot, spooning a small amount of meat sauce over each portion.
    Chef's note
    Keep the meat sauce fluid enough to hydrate the pasta layers as the lasagna bakes. Drain the fiordilatte thoroughly before assembly so it does not release excess water into the dish.

    Possible Alterations & Benefits

    Roast or air-fry the fried component instead of deep-frying — reduces oil absorption while preserving browning and crisp edges.
    Reduce the finishing butter, cheese, or rich dairy by about 20% and use cooking liquid to maintain body — keeps the dish creamy while making it less heavy.
    Use a smaller amount of cured or fatty meat and increase vegetables or aromatics — preserves savory depth while reducing heaviness and salt.
