CLASSICAL FRENCH TECHNIQUE

The Five Mother Sauces & Their Daughter Sauces

A mother sauce is a foundational preparation that can be changed with cheese, cream, herbs, wine, tomato, reductions, or other flavorings to create a family of daughter sauces. Use this guide as a map: start with the mother, then follow the links to its daughters.

How the families work

Mother sauce → daughter sauce: the daughter begins with the mother sauce and changes its flavor or character through additional ingredients or technique.

Mother → daughter → granddaughter: some sauces branch again. A classic example is Hollandaise → Béarnaise → Choron or Foyot.

MOTHER SAUCE 1

Béchamel (White Sauce)

A smooth white sauce made from milk thickened with a white roux. Its neutral, creamy character makes it the foundation for cheese, cream, onion, and mustard sauces.

Open Béchamel recipe →
FoundationMilk + white roux

Typical usesGratins, lasagna, vegetables, baked pasta, croquettes, soufflés, and cheese sauces.

Daughter sauces from Béchamel

MornayBéchamel enriched with cheese, traditionally Gruyère and/or Parmesan. SoubiseBéchamel combined with cooked or puréed onions. Crème SauceBéchamel enriched with cream. Mustard SauceBéchamel seasoned with mustard. Cheddar Cheese SauceA cheese-sauce branch built from Béchamel.

MOTHER SAUCE 2

Velouté (Velvety Stock Sauce)

A blond-roux sauce made with a light stock—commonly chicken, veal, or fish. The stock chosen determines which daughter sauces and dishes it best supports.

Open Velouté recipe →
FoundationLight stock + blond roux

Typical usesPoultry, veal, seafood, vegetables, soups, and refined cream sauces.

Daughter sauces from Velouté

AllemandeVeal velouté finished with egg yolk, cream, and lemon. SuprêmeChicken velouté enriched with cream. NantuaSeafood-oriented velouté finished with cream and shellfish flavor. NormandeFish velouté enriched with cream and seafood/fish cooking liquor. BercyFish velouté flavored with shallot and white wine. PouletteVelouté finished with mushrooms, cream, lemon, and parsley. HungarianVelouté flavored with paprika, onion, and cream. VenetianVelouté finished with herbs and complementary aromatics.

MOTHER SAUCE 3

Espagnole (Brown Sauce)

A deeply flavored brown sauce based on brown stock, brown roux, mirepoix, and tomato. In classical cooking it is commonly refined into demi-glace, which becomes a major branching point for brown daughter sauces.

Open Espagnole recipe →
FoundationBrown stock + brown roux + mirepoix + tomato

Typical usesRoasted and braised beef, veal, lamb, game, and other rich meat preparations.
Important branch: Espagnole is traditionally combined with additional brown stock and reduced to make demi-glace. Many classical brown sauces are then made from demi-glace rather than directly from Espagnole.

MOTHER SAUCE 4

Sauce Tomate (Tomato Sauce)

The classical tomato mother sauce builds depth from tomatoes, aromatics, and traditionally stock and/or pork elements. Its daughter family changes character through herbs, peppers, onions, garlic, wine, and regional seasonings.

Open Sauce Tomate recipe →
FoundationTomato + aromatics + classical flavor base

Typical usesPasta, vegetables, braises, seafood, meat, eggs, and composed sauces.

Daughter sauces from Sauce Tomate

CreoleTomato sauce with peppers, onions, celery, herbs, and regional seasoning. PortugueseTomato sauce accented with onion, garlic, and parsley. Spanish TomatoTomato sauce with peppers, onions, mushrooms, and aromatic seasoning. ProvençaleTomato sauce with garlic, herbs, and Provençal aromatics. MarinaraA simpler tomato, garlic, and herb sauce related to the tomato family. NeapolitanTomato sauce in the southern Italian tradition with garlic and herbs. AuroraA pink tomato-and-cream style sauce; classically produced by adding tomato to a pale sauce.

MOTHER SAUCE 5

Hollandaise (Butter & Egg-Yolk Emulsion)

A warm emulsion of egg yolks and clarified butter balanced with acid. Its daughters are created largely by changing the reduction, herbs, citrus, or other finishing flavors.

Open Hollandaise recipe →
FoundationEgg yolk + clarified butter + acid

Typical usesEggs, asparagus and vegetables, fish, shellfish, steak, and composed brunch dishes.

Daughter sauces from Hollandaise

BéarnaiseHollandaise family sauce flavored with a shallot-tarragon-vinegar reduction. MaltaiseHollandaise flavored with blood orange juice and zest.
Béarnaise branches again:
Hollandaise → BéarnaiseBéarnaise → Choron (tomato)Béarnaise → Foyot (meat glaze)
Quick memory aid: Béchamel = milk; Velouté = light stock; Espagnole = brown stock; Tomate = tomato; Hollandaise = egg yolk and butter. Once you know the base, the daughter sauce is easier to understand because you can see exactly what was added or changed.
