Vincisgrassi

This festive baked pasta is a hallmark of the Marche. Its precise origins are debated. One long-standing tradition links its name to Austrian general Windisch-Graetz after the 1799 defense of Ancona, while culinary historians emphasize centuries of regional evolution and adaptation.

Preparation

Vincisgrassi

Marche-style layered pasta with Marsala-enriched sheets, meat and chicken-giblet ragù, béchamel, and Grana Padano.
Yield: 6 servings
Ingredients
For the pasta sheets
2 3/4 cups plus 1 tbsp type 00 flour
1 1/4 cups fine durum-wheat semolina
4 eggs
2 tbsp dry Marsala or vino cotto
1 1/2 tbsp softened butter
3/4 tsp fine salt
For the ragù
1 1/8 lb mixed veal and pork, knife-chopped or coarsely ground
2/3 lb cleaned chicken giblets, cut into pieces
1 tbsp minced lard
2 tbsp extra-virgin olive oil
1 onion
1 celery stalk
1 carrot
3 whole cloves
1 bouquet garni made with 1 bay leaf, 2 parsley sprigs, and 1 thyme sprig
3/4 cup plus 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp dry white wine
3/4 cup plus 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp whole milk
2 tbsp tomato paste
3/4 cup plus 1 tbsp plus 1 tsp tomato passata
2 cups plus 1 tbsp plus 2 tsp hot water
1 tsp fine salt
1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
For the béchamel
3 tbsp butter
1/3 cup type 00 flour
2 cups plus 1 tbsp plus 2 tsp whole milk
2/3 tsp fine salt
1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
For assembly
1 1/2 tbsp butter
3/4 cup finely grated Grana Padano
For cooking the pasta
5 1/4 qt water
2 tbsp fine salt

Preparation

1. Make the pasta dough. Combine the type 00 flour and semolina on the work surface. Form a well and add the 4 eggs, Marsala, softened butter, and salt.
2. Knead vigorously for 10 minutes until smooth and firm. Form into a ball, wrap, and rest for 30 minutes.
3. Roll the dough between 1/16 and 3/16 inch thick and cut it into rectangles sized for the baking dish.
4. Make the ragù. Stud the onion with the 3 cloves. Cut the onion, celery, and carrot into 2–3 large pieces so they can be removed at the end of cooking.
5. Heat the lard and olive oil in a wide pot. Add the vegetables, mixed meat, and chicken giblets. Brown over medium-high heat for 10 minutes.
6. Add 1 teaspoon salt and 1/2 teaspoon pepper. Pour in the white wine and cook for 3 minutes.
7. Add the warm milk. Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 30 minutes.
8. Add the tomato paste, passata, and bouquet garni. Pour in the hot water, stir, and bring to a gentle simmer.
9. Cook uncovered over low heat until the ragù has reduced by about one-third, 60–75 minutes. Remove the onion, celery, carrot, and bouquet garni. The sauce should remain soft and fairly fluid.
10. Make the béchamel. Melt the butter in a saucepan over low heat. Add the flour and whisk for 2 minutes.
11. Heat the milk, then whisk it gradually into the roux. Add the salt, pepper, and nutmeg. Cook over low heat for 10 minutes, stirring, until creamy but still pourable.
12. Bring the water and salt to a boil. Blanch the pasta rectangles a few at a time for 2–3 minutes. Remove with a slotted spoon and lay flat on a clean kitchen towel.
13. Grease a rectangular baking dish with the butter reserved for assembly.
14. Spread a thin layer of ragù over the bottom. Cover with pasta, then add béchamel, ragù, and part of the Grana.
15. Repeat in the same order until there are 8 pasta layers, distributing the ragù, béchamel, and cheese evenly.
16. Finish with a small amount of béchamel, ragù, and a more generous portion of the remaining Grana.
17. Cover and refrigerate the assembled dish for 2 hours.
18. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Bake for 40 minutes.
19. Rest at room temperature for 10 minutes before cutting.

Service

Cut into 6 portions and serve hot.

Chef’s Note

The ragù should remain soft and slightly fluid. If it is reduced too far, it will not hydrate all eight pasta layers properly during baking.

Possible Alterations & Benefits

Reduce the finishing butter, cheese, or rich dairy by about 20% and use cooking liquid to maintain body — keeps the dish creamy while making it less heavy.
Use a smaller amount of cured or fatty meat and increase vegetables or aromatics — preserves savory depth while reducing heaviness and salt.
