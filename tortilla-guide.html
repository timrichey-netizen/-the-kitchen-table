Guide to Making Tortillas

A practical guide to soft, flexible corn and flour tortillas: dough hydration, pressing and rolling, comal temperature, the tortilla puff, storage, reheating, and choosing the right tortilla for the dish.

Corn Tortillas

Basic ratio: 2 cups masa harina + about 1½ cups warm water + ½ teaspoon salt. Makes about 16 tortillas.

  1. Mix masa harina and salt. Add warm water gradually until the masa is soft, smooth, and moist but not sticky.
  2. Cover and rest 15–30 minutes so the masa fully hydrates.
  3. Divide into golf-ball-size portions and keep covered.
  4. Press between two sheets of plastic in a tortilla press to roughly 5–6 inches.
  5. Cook on a thoroughly preheated dry comal or cast-iron skillet: about 40–50 seconds on the first side, 40–60 seconds on the second, then flip once more.
  6. When properly hydrated and heated, the tortilla should inflate or partially puff. Transfer immediately to a towel-lined tortilla warmer.

Flour Tortillas

Basic ratio: 3 cups all-purpose flour + 1 teaspoon salt + 1 teaspoon baking powder + ⅓ cup lard or shortening + about 1 cup warm water.

  1. Whisk flour, salt, and baking powder. Work in lard until evenly distributed.
  2. Add warm water and knead until smooth and supple, about 5 minutes.
  3. Divide into balls, cover, and rest at least 30 minutes. Resting is essential for easy rolling.
  4. Roll each ball paper-thin on a lightly floured surface, rotating frequently to keep it round.
  5. Cook on a very hot dry comal until bubbles form and brown spots appear, roughly 30–45 seconds per side.
  6. Stack immediately under a clean towel so trapped steam keeps them flexible.

Getting the Masa Right

The most important variable in corn tortillas is hydration. If the pressed edges crack, knead in water a teaspoon at a time. If the masa sticks badly to the plastic, work in a little masa harina. Properly hydrated masa releases cleanly and presses with relatively smooth edges.

The Comal

Preheat cast iron, carbon steel, or a traditional comal before cooking. It should be hot enough to set the tortilla quickly without scorching it. A pale, dry tortilla usually means the pan is too cool; heavy blackening before the center cooks means it is too hot.

Why Tortillas Puff

The first two passes on the comal seal the surfaces while steam develops inside. On the final flip, that steam can separate the two cooked surfaces and inflate the tortilla. A full puff is desirable, but a tortilla can still be excellent without completely ballooning.

Press vs. Rolling Pin

Press corn tortillas; roll flour tortillas. A press produces even corn tortillas without overworking the masa. Flour dough contains gluten and normally needs to be rolled thin after a good rest.

Keep Them Soft

Fresh tortillas begin losing moisture immediately. Stack them as they leave the comal and keep them enclosed in a clean kitchen towel or tortilla warmer. The residual steam finishes softening the stack.

Storage & Reheating

Cool completely before refrigerating in an airtight bag. Reheat on a dry hot skillet rather than in an oven. For several tortillas, wrap the warmed stack in a towel. Tortillas also freeze well with parchment between portions.

Which Tortilla?

  • Corn: tacos, enchiladas, chilaquiles, enfrijoladas, entomatadas, tostadas, huevos rancheros, and many quesadillas.
  • Flour: especially useful for northern-Mexican tacos, burritos, fajitas, machaca, and larger wraps.
  • Fresh masa: ideal when available; masa harina is the most practical pantry alternative.

Troubleshooting

  • Cracked edges: masa is too dry.
  • Sticks to plastic: masa is too wet or the press liner is damaged.
  • Doesn't puff: check hydration and comal temperature; avoid pressing too thick.
  • Hard tortillas: overcooked, too dry, or not held under a towel after cooking.
  • Flour dough springs back: let it rest longer before rolling.
