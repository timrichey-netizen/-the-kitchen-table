COOKING GUIDE

How to Cook Mushrooms

The key to great mushrooms is simple: dry them well, give them room, use enough heat, and let their moisture cook away before chasing a deep brown crust.

THE GOLDEN RULE

Brown first. Sauce second.

Mushrooms contain a great deal of water. In a crowded or cool pan they steam in their own liquid. For deeper flavor, cook them in a wide hot pan and wait until the released moisture evaporates before adding cream, wine, stock, or other liquids.

Before You Cook

  1. Choose firm mushrooms. They should look plump and dry rather than slimy, shriveled, or heavily bruised.
  2. Clean quickly. Brush off dirt or wipe with a damp towel. A brief rinse is fine when needed, but dry them thoroughly afterward.
  3. Trim only what is tough. Most stems are edible. Remove the dry end; discard very woody stems.
  4. Cut evenly. Similar-size pieces brown at roughly the same rate. Thick slices retain more texture than paper-thin slices.

Best Sautéing Method

  1. Heat a wide skillet over medium-high to high heat until properly hot.
  2. Add enough oil, clarified butter, or a combination of oil and butter to lightly coat the pan.
  3. Add mushrooms in a single loose layer. If the pan is crowded, cook in batches.
  4. Leave them alone long enough to brown on the first side before stirring.
  5. Continue cooking as they release water. Let that liquid evaporate rather than pouring it off.
  6. When the pan is nearly dry and the mushrooms begin browning again, add aromatics such as shallot, garlic, thyme, sage, or rosemary.
  7. Finish with salt and pepper to taste, then deglaze or add sauce ingredients if the recipe calls for them.

Common Problems & Fixes

Pale and watery

Cause: The pan is crowded or not hot enough.

Fix: Use a wider pan, raise the heat, and cook in batches.

Mushrooms absorbing all the fat

This is normal at first. Keep cooking. As the mushrooms release moisture, the pan will loosen and the fat will redistribute.

Burned garlic before mushrooms brown

Add garlic late—after the mushrooms have given up most of their water and have started browning.

Rubbery mushrooms

Avoid stopping halfway through the moisture-release stage. Keep cooking until the liquid is gone and browning resumes.

Method by Dish

For creamy sauces: Brown the mushrooms first, then add shallot, wine, stock, and cream. This keeps the sauce savory rather than watery.

For stews and braises: Sauté mushrooms separately and add them near the end when you want them to keep their shape and browned flavor.

For stir-fries: Use very high heat and small batches so the mushrooms sear quickly without flooding the wok.

For pasta: Cook the mushrooms until deeply browned before adding pasta water, miso, cream, butter, or cheese.

For roasting: Toss with oil, spread in one layer, and roast around 425°F until browned at the edges; avoid piling them on the tray.
